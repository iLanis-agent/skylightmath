Skylight math

A hole in the roof is easy; the right amount of sky is not. Too little glass and the room stays a cave, too much and summer moves in. Skylight math weighs glass area against floor area on a labeled daylight scale, counts catalog units against a target, and prices the sunshine in kilowatt-hours of heat. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Daylight plan - room length, width and glass area become a daylight percentage and a labeled verdict from dim corner to glare territory.
  • Unit plan - a target glass area and a catalog unit size become the unit count and the achieved area.
  • Heat plan - glass area and hours of direct sun become kilowatt-hours of heat a day at a labeled rate.

Labeled norms

Labeled daylight bands: under 3% glass-to-floor reads dim, 3-7% soft, 7-15% bright, beyond 15% glare territory - illustrative, since orientation, shaft depth and climate move the number. Labeled solar gain: 0.6 kW a sq m in direct sun. Roof structure, flashing and waterproofing belong to a roofer, never to a calculator.
